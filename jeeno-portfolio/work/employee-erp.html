Employee ERP · Screen Upgrade Case Study

New Screens, and Not One Field Lost

Old Windows back-office screens are moving to a web app. The look and layout can change, but the fields hold the real work, so every label, type, and order stays as it is in the old system.

The product name, logo, company, customers, and all real records are withheld. Every screen on this page was rebuilt for this portfolio with sample data, so it shows the design approach, not the product file.

01 — Overview

A screen full of fields is a screen full of rules

Officers at public agencies and private companies use this system for finance and back-office records. The example on this page is a transfer record: who gets paid, how much, from which account, and who signs. A field that looks unnecessary may be there for an audit or an accounting rule, so a redesign cannot judge it by its looks.

07 — Decisions & Trade-offs

What each rule gave, and what it cost

1. Keep every field, even the awkward ones

The old screen is the source of truth. Layout can change, but no field is added, removed, or renamed by design.

Upside

Officers keep the words they know, and no rule hidden in a field goes missing.

Cost

The screen stays dense. Simplifying it further would be a separate decision with the people who own the process.

2. Pin the columns that matter

With 14 columns, the row number, the clearing number, and the one editable amount stay in view while the rest scroll.

Upside

While scrolling, you can always see which row you are on and what is being paid.

Cost

Pinned columns take space from the scrolling area, so fewer other columns show at once.

3. Give signing a small place

Signing has the lowest priority on this screen, so it sits in a narrow rail at the bottom right, and the form and table get the room.

Upside

The main task, checking amounts, gets the most space.

Cost

Long signing histories need their own view, because the rail cannot show everything.

4. Mark guesses and ask

A screenshot cannot show a formula. Anything inferred gets a "?" in the field table and waits for a real answer.

Upside

Invented behavior does not slip into a system that handles money.

Cost

It is slower, and some screens wait on answers before the design can be finished.

08 — Where It Stands

What is done, and what still is not

01

A repeatable method

The steps and control mapping are written down, so each new module starts the same way.

02

Questions still open

Some screens still carry "?" marks, such as formulas and which fields are required, and wait for answers.

03

No impact numbers to share

This page does not claim results from officers. Its claim is about the method: new screens without lost fields.
